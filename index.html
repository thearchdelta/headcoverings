This was a somewhat tough one. I started at one position, realized there was another, very valid way to look at it, realized support for the alternative view wasn't as strong as I'd thought, came back to my first view.

Now I commend you because you remember me in everything and maintain the traditions even as I delivered them to you. 3 But I want you to understand that the head of every man is Christ, the head of a wife is her husband, and the head of Christ is God.

It begins strong. First it sets up a cosmic hierarchy that is timeless and indepentent of culture.

4 Every man who prays or prophesies with his head covered dishonors his head, 5 but every wife who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonors her head, since it is the same as if her head were shaven.

Question: if the objective is for the man to honor Christ and for the woman to honor her husband, how is it best done? Does Paul say, "Let us look around at the surrounding culture and see how they do it, so we, the church, can be seen by the public as expressing and honoring these eternal hierarchies"?

Were his commandments merely reflections of the practices of Corinth at large? Maybe. It wouldn't be a bad idea, and God has used this pattern before. So I did a little bit of research, which included the link you sent, the link I sent you, and Grok.

The results? Inconclusive.

For women, there are conflicting reports. Some say bareheadedness in public was shameful, some say it was commonplace. Some say the temple prostitutes of Corinth shaved their heads. The link I sent you insists that that practice had been abolished two centuries prior.

The history is a fog. It's a little clearer for men. Men, apparently, did indeed cover their heads during official acts in the course of temple worship. In that case, Paul was going directly against the prevailing culture.

Bottom line, I am reluctant to believe in my own hypothesis: that Paul was using local norms to illustrate eternal piety.

So back I went to the Biblical text, and the impression that Paul reasoning from fundamental, eternal truths, rather than societal mores, only grew stronger:

For a man ought not to cover his head, since he is the image and glory of God, but woman is the glory of man. 8 For man was not made from woman, but woman from man. 9 Neither was man created for woman, but woman for man. 10 That is why a wife ought to have a symbol of authority on her head, because of the angels.

And stronger still...

14 Does not nature itself teach you that if a man wears long hair it is a disgrace for him, 15 but if a woman has long hair, it is her glory? For her hair is given to her for a covering

...since Paul is now appealing to Nature itself, which is independent of time, place and people.

About the angel business

Let me quickly comment about the angel business. I think the instructive scriptural reference here is Jude vs 9.

Remember when you were a kid and you said "Shut up!" to your little brother, and your parents rebuked you? Neither "shut" nor "up" is a bad word. What they objected to was the somewhat disrespectful liberty of using harsh, abusive language towards your brother in their presence.

I believe this is what is going on here:

9 But when [even] the archangel Michael, contending with the devil, judicially argued (disputed) about the body of Moses, he dared not [presume to] bring an abusive condemnation against him, but [simply] said, The Lord rebuke you!

It would appear that the angels have a keenly developed sense of propriety.

Some final points:

01

The text refers to wives. I have no idea what this means for unmarried women.

02

The text refers to covering (or uncovering) when praying or prophesying, and doing so in public, keeping in mind the definition of prophesying we learnt from Pastor Matt.

03

In the part of Africa where I grew up, women struggle mortally with hair. It is by no means to be assumed that it's natural for a woman to have longer hair than a man.

04

Absalom, the most handsome man in the Bible, had really long hair. Really long.

2 Samuel 14:25–26 (ESV): “Now in all Israel there was no one so much to be praised for his handsome appearance as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him. And when he cut the hair of his head (for at the end of every year he used to cut it; when it was heavy on him, he cut it), he weighed the hair of his head, two hundred shekels by the king’s weight.”

Did they consider Absalom to be a disgrace? Perhaps it was indeed a scandal, but he was the dashing, charming, handsome prince. Of all people, he could pull it off. Who knows?
